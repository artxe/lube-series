- The tests must also pass with `TZ` set to `America/New_York`, `Australia/Lord_Howe` (half hour DST) and `America/Santiago` (DST change at midnight); the `verify` skill has the Windows command.
- Scope (owner): the platform's `Date` plus what is painful to write by hand. No text for people (month names, AM and PM, relative time are `Intl`'s job) and no comparison helpers (`a < b` and `b - a` do it).
- Format tokens follow the ECMAScript date string `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm:ss.sss` (owner's decision): never add the `SSS` of moment, dayjs and date-fns, not even as an alias.
- `src/zone.js` holds the only `Intl` use, an offset read from `timeZoneName: "longOffset"`: cheaper than numeric parts, and it keeps the seconds of historical offsets. `fields_of` and `date_of` there convert between a `Date` and wall clock fields; every other file goes through them.
- A wall clock in a gap moves forward and one in an overlap takes the first occurrence, as `new Date(y, m, d, h)` does. `instant_of` gets there by reading the offset a day before the wall time first; guessing at the wall time itself flips the direction with the sign of the offset (backward in America/*, second occurrence in Europe/*).
- `endOf` builds the start of the next unit from fields; start plus one unit is wrong when the unit begins with a gap (America/Santiago, Asia/Seoul 1950). A day or week that starts inside a gap starts at the transition (America/Toronto 1919).
- `startOf` and `endOf` of `"H"`, `"m"`, `"s"` move by elapsed time and keep the result only while its offset equals the input's; without a zone compare `getTimezoneOffset` and the seconds field, since `getTimezoneOffset` drops the seconds of historical offsets. Otherwise `unit_start` and `unit_end` in `src/zone.js` walk transition by transition: a forward jump inside the unit is crossed, a backward jump or a jump into another unit ends it, so a unit never exceeds its length and `endOf + 1` is the next `startOf`, also when only part of an hour repeats (Europe/Paris 1911, Australia/Lord_Howe, Pacific/Chatham).
- Without a zone the results inherit V8's local `Date`, which caches a stale offset after some historical transitions (Europe/Tirane 1943, Africa/Tunis 1943, Africa/El_Aaiun 1976, Asia/Gaza 2040, and V8 disagrees with Intl for Brazil 2000 and Europe/Vienna 1943–45); a mismatch there between the local and the zone argument paths is not a library bug.
- `diff` of `"M"` and `"Y"` counts the largest k whose anchor (the first date's day and time moved k months, a missing day clamped to the month's last day as `add` does, so `diff` inverts `add`) is reached. Fuzz it against `add` of `@js-temporal/polyfill`, not against Temporal's `until`, which compares the unclamped day, nor node's `--harmony-temporal`. The anchor is resolved like `add` only when its wall clock is within two days of the second date.
- `test/datetime-lube/temporal.test.js` loads `@js-temporal/polyfill` with `createRequire` and types it with the lib's global `Temporal`: importing it pulls in its `index.d.ts`, which conflicts with TypeScript's `esnext.temporal` lib under `--skipLibCheck false`.
- `diff` of `"D"` and `"W"` subtracts calendar day numbers, never elapsed midnights: Pacific/Apia skipped 2011-12-30.
- Benchmark-tuned, keep: format and duration strings compile once into a cached array, and each cache (also the zone formatters, which spellings of one zone share by `resolvedOptions().timeZone`) is cleared when it is full so distinct strings never grow it without bound; `add`, `startOf` and `endOf` work on a `Date` directly when no time zone is given; month arithmetic uses a days-in-month table instead of extra `Date` objects; with a time zone, wall clock fields and wall milliseconds are pure arithmetic in `src/calendar.js`, valid at the ends of the `Date` range, not a `Date` round trip. Count the `Intl` offset reads: `date_of` needs two, three only within a day after a transition.
